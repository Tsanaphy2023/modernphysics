# Evidence Notes: Nanotechnological Physics (4014971)

## Document-level evidence

- The document is a course specification (TQF 3) for **4014971 Nanotechnological Physics** in the Physics program.
- The course description focuses on nanotechnology: meaning and scope; nanoscale measurement; nanomaterial synthesis; instruments for synthesis and characterization; applications; inquiry and laboratory practice; and use in learning management.
- Stated learning results after instruction:
  1. Understand physics principles and explain phenomena.
  2. Analyze using learned physics principles.
  3. Study and analyze problems and propose creative, systematic solutions that consider relevant theory and decision consequences.
  4. Take responsibility for assigned individual and group work.
  5. Use statistical or mathematical techniques effectively for investigation and problem solving.
- In the learning-outcome matrix, the course is formally mapped to ethics (1.2), knowledge (2.1, 2.2, 2.4), and numerical/data-analysis, communication, and IT skills (5.1, 5.2). It has no stated mappings in intellectual-skills domain 3 or interpersonal-responsibility domain 4.
- Knowledge teaching/assessment uses lectures, videos/online media, inquiry/presentations, lab work, discussion, homework, and examinations. Ethics uses behaviour/attendance/responsibility observation. Domain 5 uses discussion and presentations.
- Assessment weighting is 10% ethics/responsibility observation, 60% midterm and final examinations for knowledge objectives, and 30% presentation and question-answer for numerical/data, communication, and IT objectives.
- A material inconsistency occurs in the section titled "objectives for course development": both listed statements refer to plasma physics and plasma technology rather than nanotechnology. The course description and teaching plan consistently refer to nanotechnology.
- The teaching plan covers nanotechnology foundations and integrated work (weeks 1–4); nanoscale measurement (weeks 4–5); nanomaterial synthesis (weeks 6–8); synthesis instruments (weeks 9–10); nanoscale material characterization (weeks 11–12); and applied nanotechnology using inquiry/lab work for learning management (weeks 13–15).

## Initial evaluation themes

- Strengths: includes knowledge, analytic/problem-solving, responsibility, quantitative/data, communication, and technology themes; provides a theory–practice intent; contains broad instructional/assessment links.
- Limitations: the central outcome statements are broad and not explicitly nanoscale-specific; conditions, observable performance standards, and success criteria are absent. The stated outcome about teamwork does not align with the blank TQF interpersonal-responsibility mapping. The stated analytical/problem-solving outcome does not align with a populated intellectual-skills mapping. The statistical/mathematical outcome has weak direct evidence in the weekly plan and is assessed mainly by presentation rather than an authentic quantitative product. Practical laboratory/characterization competence is implied by the course description but is not specified as an assessable performance outcome. Teaching plan assessments rely heavily on observation, question-answer, and discussion, with limited rubrics or criteria disclosed.
