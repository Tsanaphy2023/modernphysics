
## AI in High School Education: The Future of Learning

- AI is increasingly integrated into education, with generative tools becoming more advanced.
- AI can support learning when used ethically, serving as a valuable resource.
- **How Students Use AI:**
    - AI tools can help break down difficult topics to strengthen understanding.
    - Can "level the playing field" for students with disabilities (e.g., computer-generated voices for visually impaired/dyslexic, word-prediction for dyslexic, generative AI for math/logic problems).
    - Examples: Duolingo uses AI for personalized lessons and custom feedback.
    - Auto-graded assignments in online high schools use AI for quick feedback.
- **Most Popular AI Tools for High School Students:**
    - ChatGPT: Versatile research and problem-solving.
    - Grammarly: Grammar and spelling checker.
    - Notion: Productivity and note-taking.
    - Google Gemini: Research tool.
    - Copyscape: Plagiarism detector.
    - Otter.ai: Transcription tool.
    - Dall-E: Digital image generator.
    - Semantic Scholar: Scientific literature research tool.
- These tools complement traditional learning methods and are effective as support, not substitutes for individual effort.
- **Teacher Training with AI:**
    - Teachers use AI-detection tools (e.g., Turnitin) to identify AI-generated assignments.
    - Benefits for teachers: saved time, personalization, data-driven decisions, accessibility for students.
    - AI can help gamify activities, design visuals, convert text to speech.
- **Future of AI in Education (World Economic Forum):**
    1. Supporting teachers' role: augmentation and automation.
    2. Refining assessment and decision-making.
    3. Supporting AI and digital literacy.
    4. Personalizing learning content and experience.
- Students learning AI responsibly will be prepared for tomorrow's workforce.
- **Final Thoughts:** AI is more than just generative AI (ChatGPT); it's used daily in search engines, voice assistants (Alexa, Siri), etc.
- Ethical concerns exist, but AI can supplement education and deepen understanding. Students should check school's AI policy.



## What Is Artificial Intelligence? Definition, Uses, and Types

- **Definition:** AI refers to computer systems capable of performing complex tasks that historically only humans could do, such as reasoning, making decisions, or solving problems.
- **Umbrella Term:** AI encompasses a wide variety of technologies, including machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing (NLP).
- **Distinction:** Some argue that much of today's AI technology is highly advanced machine learning, a first step towards true artificial intelligence (General Artificial Intelligence - GAI).
- **Common Usage:** When most people use the term AI today, they refer to machine learning-powered technologies (e.g., ChatGPT, computer vision) that enable machines to perform human-like tasks (generating content, steering a car, analyzing data).
- **AI stands for "artificial intelligence":** The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, such as computer systems.
- **Applications:** AI powers many technology-driven industries, such as health care, finance, transportation, and much more.



## The History of Artificial Intelligence

- Humans have long dreamed of creating thinking machines, reflected in folklore and historical attempts to build programmable devices.
- **GPT-3** (Generative Pretrained Transformer) was a landmark moment in AI due to its unprecedented size (175 billion parameters), enabling a wide range of natural language tasks without extensive fine-tuning. It could generate human-like text and engage in conversations, and perform few-shot learning.
- AI is increasingly embedded in daily life, from social media to work processes.

### Pre-20th century
- **1726:** Jonathan Swift's "Gulliver's Travels" introduces "The Engine," a mechanical contraption to generate new ideas and texts by combining words. This anticipates algorithmic text generation.

### 1900–1950
- **1914:** Spanish engineer Leonardo Torres y Quevedo demonstrates _El Ajedrecista_, the first fully automated chess-playing machine using electromagnets. It could play a simple chess endgame autonomously.
- **1921:** Karel Čapek's play "Rossum's Universal Robots" (R.U.R) introduces the word "robot" (from Czech "robota" meaning forced work). The term became associated with mechanical, humanoid machines for monotonous labor.
- **1939:** John Vincent Atanasoff and Clifford Berry create the Atanasoff-Berry Computer (ABC), considered one of the earliest digital electronic computers. It introduced key concepts: binary system, electronic circuits for computation, and separation of data storage from processing unit.
- **1943:** Warren S. McCulloch and Walter Pitts publish "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity," laying the foundation for understanding the brain as a computational system and introducing artificial neural networks.
- **1950:** Alan Turing's paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" introduces the Turing Test (or "imitation game") to measure machine intelligence by assessing its ability to mimic human conversation and behavior indistinguishably.

### 1950–1980
- **1951:** Marvin Minsky and Dean Edmunds build SNARC (Stochastic Neural Analog Reinforcement Calculator), the first artificial neural network, simulating learning processes through reinforcement learning (mimicking how animals learn via rewards/punishment).
- **1952:** Allen Newell and Herbert A. Simon develop programs like Logic Theorist and General Problem Solver, mimicking human problem-solving.
- **1955:** The term "artificial intelligence" is coined in a workshop proposal for the "Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence" by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, and Claude Shannon. This workshop (1956) is considered the official birthdate of the field.



## Everyday examples and applications of artificial intelligence (AI)

- AI is pervasive in everyday life; a survey showed that while only 33% think they use AI, over 77% use an AI-powered service or device.
- **What is Artificial Intelligence?**
    - A specific branch of computer science focused on replicating human thought processes and decision-making through algorithms.
    - Different branches of AI exist, from simple task completion to complex programs that adjust their own algorithms or beat human experts.
- **Examples and applications of AI:**
    - **Digital assistants:** Siri, Alexa, Cortana, Google Assistant, Bixby.
    - **Search engines:** Google, Yahoo, Bing, DuckDuckGo (use AI for refining results, auto-complete, "People also ask").
    - **Social media:** Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok (AI-powered algorithms determine content, gather user data for advertising).
    - **Online shopping:** Personalized product recommendations, pricing optimization, chatbots, shipping/delay estimates.
    - **Robots:** Used in aerospace (Mars rovers), manufacturing (assembly lines), hospitality (check-in, drink mixing, meal delivery).
    - **Transportation and navigation:** Self-driving cars, map software (Google Maps, Apple Maps, Waze) for real-time traffic, ETAs, rideshare apps, AI-powered autopilot in aircraft.
    - **Text editing and autocorrect:** Software that learns from dictionaries, common use, and user patterns (e.g., autocorrect, grammar checkers).
    - **Fraud prevention:** AI analyzes patterns in transactions to detect and prevent fraudulent activities.
    - **Predictions:** AI models are used to forecast trends in various fields, from weather to stock markets.
    - **Gaming:** AI powers non-player characters (NPCs), adapts game difficulty, and creates realistic game environments.
    - **Healthcare:** AI assists in diagnosis, drug discovery, personalized treatment plans, and medical imaging analysis.
    - **Advertising:** AI optimizes ad targeting, campaign management, and content creation for marketing.
    - **Analytics:** AI helps process and interpret large datasets to derive insights and support decision-making in businesses.
