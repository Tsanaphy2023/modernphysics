
## How to help students use AI ethically (Carnegie Learning)

- AI chatbots are large language models, not interactive encyclopedias. Students should not thoughtlessly rely on them due to potential bias and inaccuracies.
- Encourage critical thinking by requiring students to ask for multiple perspectives from AI or to use AI only after learning about a topic with verified sources.

### Bias in AI:
- AI-generated content can reflect biases from the human-generated content it was trained on, leading to harmful ideas, omitted information, and perpetuated stereotypes.
- *Example:* A Bloomberg report found an AI image generator produced images of light-skinned people in high-paying jobs and dark-skinned people in low-paying jobs.
- Students need to develop a critical eye to spot biases in AI output.

### Accuracy of AI:
- Generative AI is imperfect, and its content needs to be verified with reputable sources.
- AI can be unreliable; for example, ChatGPT's accuracy in math problems has significantly decreased.
- AI can "hallucinate" information, inventing book titles, legal cases, or giving dangerous health advice.
- Students should be aware of these limitations and cross-reference AI answers with trustworthy sources.
