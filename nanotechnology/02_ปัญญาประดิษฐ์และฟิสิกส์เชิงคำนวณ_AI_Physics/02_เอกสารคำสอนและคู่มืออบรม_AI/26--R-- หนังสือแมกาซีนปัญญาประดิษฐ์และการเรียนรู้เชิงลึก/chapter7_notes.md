
## Agriculture: Harnessing AI for healthier soils

- Soil health is determined by organic matter content, porosity, nutrient content, pH, erosion resistance, water management, and biotic interactions.
- AI and Machine Learning (ML) are supplementing conventional approaches for assessing and evaluating farming practices.
- AI techniques are highly accurate in making soil texture predictions.

### How do AI and ML techniques work?
- AI and ML collect data from extensive soil sampling and historical data to create comprehensive predictive soil maps.
- Traditionally, soil health determination (sieving, sedimentation, analysis) is time-consuming, sometimes inaccurate, and requires skilled professionals.
- AI can process enormous amounts of data, link it to historical soil data to establish patterns and relationships, and make predictions or classifications.
- **Applications:** Predict nutrient levels, determine soil moisture, assess soil erosion risk, classify soil types.
- When combined with remote sensing (satellite imagery, airborne sensors), AI becomes even more efficient and reliable.
- AI can process non-numerical data (images, videos, text, voice) with greater perfection than the human brain.

### AI technologies in agriculture: The benefits
- Farmers and land managers can make data-driven decisions, optimize resource use, and implement practices that protect and enhance soil health.
- **Applications of AI in agriculture:**
    - Monitoring and digitizing soil data for informed decisions on water and nutrient use.
    - Pest control (predicting pesticide application times, identifying potential pests).
    - Risk management and precision forecasting (predicting variables like plant strain, soil data, irrigation, supply chain).
    - Planning for precise application of fertilizers, pesticides, and irrigation.
    - Locating carbon sinks and increasing carbon capture in soil.
- **Examples:** USDA NRCS funding a study using AI-based radar for root-zone soil moisture measurement (could increase irrigation efficiency by 50%). The Missouri Partnership with Climate FieldViewTM provides data for real-time farm decisions.

### Challenges for AI to determine soil health
- **Cost:** AI technology is expensive and not easily accessible to smaller farms.
- **Expertise:** Farmers need knowledge and expertise to handle and understand AI systems, requiring training and time investment.
- **Data Management:** Managing, storing, and securing data may conflict with data privacy laws.
- Need for international collaboration, regulatory cooperation, and open sharing of scientific data.

### The future of AI in agriculture
- AI and ML are game-changers in agriculture, with the global market projected to grow significantly.
- They will play a pivotal role in sustainable soil management practices to feed a growing population.
